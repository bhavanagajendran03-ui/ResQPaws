      First aid while you travel

      General guidance only. A vet should always examine the animal.

      Bleeding
      • Press a clean cloth firmly on the wound.
      • If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top instead of removing it.
      • Keep the animal calm and head to a vet.
      Heatstroke
      • Move the animal to shade or a cool room.
      • Wet the body with cool, not ice-cold, water and offer small sips.
      • Go to a vet right away, even if the animal seems better.
      Suspected poisoning
      • Note what was eaten and roughly how much, and keep the packaging.
      • Don't make the animal vomit unless a vet tells you to.
      • Call a vet immediately.
      Choking
      • Look in the mouth, and remove an object only if you can see it and it is safe to do so.
      • Don't push your fingers blindly down the throat.
      • Get to a vet urgently.
      Road accident
      • Make sure the area is safe for you first.
      • Move the animal as little as possible. Slide a blanket or board under it if you must.
      • Injured animals may bite, so approach slowly and take care.

          How to help safely

          • Keep yourself safe. Frightened or hurt animals may bite or scratch.
          • Give space. Keep the animal calm and away from traffic if you can do so safely.
          • Skip food and water for an injured animal until a vet or volunteer advises.
          • Stay reachable so volunteers can call you for directions.
